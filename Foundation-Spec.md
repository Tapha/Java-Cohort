## 1. PRODUCT TRUTH

A mobile app that turns a quick photo of your available ingredients into a clear, appealing meal you can cook right now, for busy people deciding whether to eat in or order out.

***

## 2. THE USER

A young professional at the moment of friction: it’s lunchtime or evening, they’re hungry, tired, and about to order food—standing in front of their fridge or scrolling their phone—seeking an easy, confidence-boosting alternative.

***

## 3. THE CORE LOOP

1. Capture: User snaps a photo of their fridge/pantry (or inputs ingredients).
2. Generate: App proposes visually appealing meal options using what they have.
3. Decide: User chooses one based on time, effort, and preference.
4. Cook (or save): User follows through or stores it for later.

Repeat the next time they’re about to order food.

***

## 4. WHY NOW

* Computer vision + generative AI can now reliably interpret ingredients and produce appealing food visuals. \[INFERRED]
* Users are conditioned by Instagram/Pinterest to choose visually first. \[STATED]
* Food delivery is ubiquitous, increasing demand for a viable “cook instead” alternative in the same decision window. \[INFERRED]

***

## 5. SUCCESS CRITERIA

* Users open the app at the exact moment they are about to order food (decision-point usage).
* A meaningful % of sessions end in selecting a suggested meal (not abandoning).
* Repeat usage within a week tied to new meal decisions.
* Users rely on photo input as the default (camera-first behavior).
* Users cook at least one meal per week they wouldn’t have otherwise made.

***

## 6. NON-GOALS

* Not a full recipe database or cooking education platform.
* Not a grocery shopping or delivery service.
* Not a social media platform (sharing is secondary, not the core loop).
* Not a calorie-tracking or fitness/nutrition tracker.
* Not an exhaustive inventory management system.

***

## 7. LOAD-BEARING ASSUMPTIONS

* Users prefer snapping a fridge photo over manually listing ingredients. \[STATED]
* Visually appealing meals (AI-generated images) increase likelihood of cooking. \[STATED]
* Users want a decision made for them, not a list of options. \[INFERRED]
* The app can suggest meals even with incomplete/limited ingredient sets. \[STATED]
* Users are open to cooking if friction is low enough at decision time. \[INFERRED]

**Critical assumption (kill shot):**

* Users will trust and act on AI-generated meal suggestions from imperfect ingredient recognition. \[INFERRED]

***

## 8. OPEN QUESTIONS

* Should the app optimize for *one* best meal vs multiple choices?
* How accurate does ingredient recognition need to be to maintain trust?
* Are AI-generated food images necessary, or would real recipe images suffice?
* How much personalization (diet, allergies, history) is required in v1?
* Should social sharing exist at launch or wait?
* How to handle missing ingredients—suggest substitutions vs “quick add to shop”?
